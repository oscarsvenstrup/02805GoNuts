Week 1 / Group exercise 1.8

Fame vs. cataloging:
the structural asymmetry
of the Marvel universe.

A directed link means two different things depending on which end you stand on. We split a Wikipedia hyperlink graph of 303 Marvel characters into who receives attention and who gives it, and asked what changes when you stop averaging the two together.

Data: 303 Marvel charactersFrozen: 26 August 2026By Oscar Svenstrup, Alessandro Perin & Victor Cabré

01 / What we asked

One link,
two meanings.

Every arrow in this graph is a Wikipedia link from one character's article to another. An in-link is an endorsement decided somewhere else, by an editor writing a different page; an out-link is a citation decided here, by whoever wrote this page. Our question: how does passive prominence — the fame of being linked to — differ in structure and scale from the active, effortful work of authoring the links out?

05 / In-links vs. out-links

Fame is a lottery.
Editing is a chore.

In-degree is heavy-tailed: a small number of characters are linked to from hundreds of separate articles, each link decided independently by a different editor. Spider-Man tops the list with 106 in-links. Out-degree tells the opposite story — it is bounded by how many peers one person can reasonably cite while writing a single page. The busiest author, Betsy Braddock, links out to only 28 other characters, close to the practical ceiling for one article.

06 / The satellites

Not every path

277 characters (91% of the cast) sit in one giant connected component — ignore the arrows and you can walk from any of them to any other. But 9 characters form a separate island with no path at all to the rest of the universe, and 17 more have no links in either direction. The edge list alone would erase all of them; loading the full node roster first is what keeps them part of the story.

07 / Conclusion

Split the arrows.
See both stories.

A directed network hides its most interesting behavior if you collapse it too early. Averaging in- and out-degree together would have buried a real structural asymmetry: being noticed scales with how many independent editors exist, while noticing is capped by what a single editor can write in one sitting. The topology is a fingerprint of that constraint — and keeping the full node roster, isolates and satellite island included, is what let us see it.

“In-links are given.
Out-links are made.”
